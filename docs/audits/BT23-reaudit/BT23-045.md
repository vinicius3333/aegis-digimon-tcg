# BT23-045 TigerVespamon ACE re-audit

Sources: committed catalog `BT23-045`; KB Q5307, Q5308, Q5309, Q5310, Q5331; comprehensive rules 15-6, 15-7, 15-9, 15-14-5.

## Card

Green/Black level 6 Mega, play cost 8, DP 12000, Virus, traits Cyborg / X Antibody / Royal Base / Zaxon / CS / Insectoid, ACE with Overflow -4. Printed clauses:

1. `[Digivolve] Lv.5 w/[Royal Base]/[CS] trait: Cost 3` — alternate route.
2. `[Hand] [Counter] ＜Blast Digivolve＞`.
3. `[On Play] [When Digivolving] By placing 1 [Royal Base] or [Zaxon] trait Digimon card from your hand or trash face up as the bottom security card, return 1 of your opponent's Digimon with as much or less DP as this Digimon to the hand.`
4. `[All Turns] When this Digimon suspends, by flipping your top face-up security card face down, 1 of your Digimon unsuspends.`

The catalog text matches the official card list. No catalog change was needed or made.

## Defect found and fixed (Q5331)

The IR modelled the by-placement as unconditionally `optional: true` with `abortOnDecline`, so the controller could always refuse it. Q5331 says the opposite: once the effect activates the controller **must** process the "by" condition; only when no [Royal Base]/[Zaxon] Digimon card sits in the (public) trash may the controller decline, because the hand is hidden.

`BT23-045.ts` now emits two mutually exclusive `Return` actions per trigger, gated on the same `selfHasMinTrash` check:

- trash holds a qualifying card → mandatory action, cost payable from hand or trash;
- trash holds none → optional action with `abortOnDecline`, cost payable from hand only.

Exactly one branch can ever resolve. The literal ruling text says the placed card must be one of the trash cards; the module keeps hand and trash both selectable in the mandatory branch, since the ruling's point is that the payment cannot be refused, and forbidding the hand choice would remove a legal choice the rules never take away.

## Fixture notes

- The turn loop's Active phase unsuspends the board, so the "suspended ally" fixtures arm `isSuspended` after seat 0's Main opens rather than in the Board Spec.
- Every fixture keeps a spare playable card in hand. Without one the engine auto-passes the Main phase and the later assertions read a state from the opponent's turn.

## Open interpretation, pinned not blessed

"Your top face-up security card" is executed as "the topmost face-up card in the stack", not "the top card, and only while it is face up". No ruling settles the wording, and the strict reading would make the clause almost dead on a card that places face-up cards at the bottom. The test `flips the topmost face-up card even when a face-down card sits above it` pins the current behavior with a comment, so a change to it must be deliberate. This is not claimed as correct.

## Observation outside this lane

In the Q5308/Q5309 fixture, the checked ST22-08 leaves the security stack to its owner's **hand** rather than the trash after its [Security] effect resolves. The behavior is identical face up and face down, so it is not a face-up-security defect and it is not BT23-045's clause; it belongs to the security-check lane. The test asserts face-up/face-down parity and the [Security] deletion instead of blessing that destination.

## Blast Digivolve fixture correction (session 2)

The original ＜Blast Digivolve＞ test drove the counter with a bare `digivolve` intent. That left the opponent's [Counter] window open and the attack unresolved, so the test proved the digivolve but not the clause's public path or its end state. Both Blast tests now follow the BT23-027 pattern: the real turn loop, seat 1 attacks, seat 0 answers with `respondCounter` and the `blast-digivolve:<permanentId>` effect key, and the test asserts the attack's end state (security stack by instance ids, attacker fate, no pending decision). The suite grew from 22 to 23 tests and stays green.
