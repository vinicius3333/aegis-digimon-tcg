### Q5165 — why it is untestable here

BT17-087 Marcus Damon grants itself `kinds: ["Digimon"]` only from its own `[On Play]`, with
duration `untilOpponentTurnEnd`, so the opponent must play it on **their** turn. The ruling's
only observable consequence is BT12-038 GeoGreymon's inherited effect, printed
`[Your Turn] [Once Per Turn] If one of your yellow or red Tamers becomes suspended, …` — "your
turn" is the Tamer controller's turn. This card can place the material only on **our** turn, and
by the Tamer controller's next turn the treated-as-Digimon grant has expired (Q5165 itself says
the inherited effects are lost at that moment). The two windows never overlap, and no public
intent suspends an opponent's Tamer on their own turn. The half that is provable is proved:
a Digimon host gains the placed card's inherited effect, a plain Tamer host does not (Q5164).
The reason is recorded in a comment at the same place in the test file.

### DigiXros evidence

- **Material sources.** Hand (both materials) and battle area (the material permanent is
  relocated whole and leaves the battle area) are proved separately.
- **Reduced cost per material.** 16 printed − 2 × 3 = 10 paid: memory 16 → 6. Proved again
  against a memory total of 0, where the printed play is rejected `insufficient-memory` and the
  DigiXros play succeeds and lands memory at −10.
- **Material cap.** A third material is rejected `invalid-material`: the recipe has exactly two
  named slots and `materialsSatisfyRecipe` refuses `materials.length > slots.length`.
- **Non-matching material refused.** A card matching no slot (BT1-009), a second [Bagramon] that
  cannot occupy the [DarkKnightmon] slot, and a trash-sourced material (this card prints no
  trash allowance and no expander Tamer is in play) are all `invalid-material`. An empty
  declaration is `no-materials`. The board is unchanged after every refusal.

## Defects and notes

- **No behavioural defect found in the module.** Every printed clause resolves correctly through
  public intents, including the three conferral orderings the KB distinguishes (Q5166 / Q5167 /
  Q5168) and the EX7-061 interrupt chain (Q5169).
- **Cosmetic IR note, not fixed.** `digiXrosRequirement[0].costReduction: 3` is dead: `count` is
  the per-material reduction whenever it is a number, and `costReduction` is read only when
  `count === "∞"` (`apps/api/src/engine/actions/digiXros.ts`, `perMaterialReduction`). It is
  harmless because both values are 3, and removing it would drift `effects.json`, which this
  lane must not touch. Flagged for the coordinator rather than changed.
- **`maxMaterials` is absent and does not need to be present.** The two distinct named slots
  already cap the play at two materials; `maxMaterials` exists for the single-slot "1 [A], 1 [B],
  or 1 [C]" form. Proved by the rejected 3-material declaration.
- No retained reds. No `it.fails` / `it.skip` in this file.
- `packages/shared/src/cards/data/cards.json` shows modified in the worktree; that is the
  coordinator's catalog fix and was not touched.

## Remaining gaps

- Q5165 is documented untestable (above) rather than proved.
- The `copyTrigger: "AllTurns"` narrowing (a level-6 [Bagra Army] stack card's `[On Play]` must
  **not** be conferred) is asserted structurally only. The previous file proved it by firing
  `[On Play]` through the injected-timing seam and reading `ledgers.subTriggers`, which earns no
  credit under this brief; a public-intent proof would need a turn loop reaching the end of the
  opponent's turn to observe EX10-058's granted `[End of Your Turn]` watcher never appearing.
- Material stacking order after a DigiXros is asserted by membership, not sequence: loose hand
  materials each land at the bottom of the growing stack, so the declared order is reversed,
  while a battle-area material is relocated and keeps declaration order. §7-2-2-7 leaves the
  order to the player, so neither is a rules violation, but the two paths are inconsistent with
  each other and a coordinator may want one convention.
