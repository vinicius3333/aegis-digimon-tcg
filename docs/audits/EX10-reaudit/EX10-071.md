# EX10-071 Paradise Lost — re-audit

Option, Purple/Yellow, play cost 2, [Seven Great Demon Lords], U, 4 per deck.

## Printed clauses (catalog)

1. **[Static]** "While you have a Digimon with [Lucemon] in its name on the field, you can
   ignore this card's color requirements."
2. **[Trash] [End of Your Turn]** "If you have a Digimon with [Lucemon] in its name, by
   returning this card to the bottom of the deck, trash your top security card and 1 of your
   Digimon attacks without suspending."
3. **[Main]** "Until your opponent's turn ends, 1 of your Digimon with [Lucemon] in its name
   gains ＜Raid＞, ＜Piercing＞, ＜Blocker＞ and +3000 DP."
4. **[Security]** "You may play 1 Digimon with [Lucemon] in its name from your trash without
   paying the cost."

There is no ＜Delay＞ on this card; the coordinator note's ＜Delay＞ item does not apply.

## Catalog discrepancies (reported, not edited)

- `effectText` doubles the phrase: "a Digimon with [Lucemon] in its name **in its name**" in
  both clause 1 and clause 2. Cosmetic mangling; the meaning is unambiguous.
- `securityEffectText` reads "You may play 1 **[Lucemon] in its name** from your trash" — the
  words "Digimon with" were dropped by the same mangling. Two things follow:
  - the name test is a SUBSTRING test ("in its name"), so `match: "name"` is right, not
    `nameExact`;
  - the clause is limited to Digimon. The compiled `PlayWithoutCost` filter had no `kind`
    gate. Fixed locally (see below). No behavioural difference exists today: every card in
    the catalog whose name contains "Lucemon" is a Digimon (12 prints, checked).

## Defect fixed locally

- `EX10-071.ts`, `Security` effect: added `kind: ["Digimon"]` to the `PlayWithoutCost` target
  filter. The printed clause plays a **Digimon**; the compiled filter matched any card kind.
  Unobservable today (no non-Digimon Lucemon print exists), so it is a fidelity fix, not a
  behaviour change. The compiled IR changed, so `packages/shared/src/effects/effects.json`
  now drifts for this card — the coordinator's sync is required.

## Weaknesses in the previous audit (all fixed)

Every clause of the committed test proved its behaviour through injected timing
(`advance().fireForInstance(EffectTiming.OnEndTurn, …)` and
`advance().fireForInstance(EffectTiming.SecuritySkill, …)`), which the brief scores 0 in the
Behavior column. Three production proofs were added:

- the trash clause now runs inside the real turn loop's End phase, reached by `endPhase`;
- Q5185 is now the actual question asked — the card is PLAYED this turn and activates from the
  trash it landed in, instead of being seeded there;
- the Security clause now fires from a real security check driven by an `attack` intent.

## Peer comparison (round 2)

The duration test also plays **EX10-014 Weatherdramon** in the same Main. Its `[On Play]` grants
＜Security A. -1＞ "until their turn ends" — the same printed duration, compiled to the same
`untilOpponentTurnEnd` ref. In one fixture both grants are asserted live during seat 1's turn and
both are gone at seat 0's next Main, so the two cards are proved to share the duration semantics
rather than each card asserting the string in isolation.

## Fixture notes for other lanes

- A board seeded `suspended: true` cannot prove Q5187 under the turn loop: seat 0's own Active
  phase unsuspends it before Main opens. Suspend it with a real attack instead.
- The two injected-timing trash tests seeded security with BT1-001, a Digi-Egg, which can never
  legally sit in a security stack. Replaced with BT1-013 Muchomon (inert main-deck Digimon);
  the `security` alias and every assertion are unchanged.
- The duration fixture runs with `autoDeclineOptional: true`. With `autoAcceptOptional` the
  Option's own [Trash] [End of Your Turn] clause pays its return cost at seat 0's turn end and
  fires an extra attack that clears seat 1's board, which destroys the block fixture on the next
  turn. Declining aborts that clause cleanly (the CR 15-7-4 test covers the declined branch).
- EX10-060 Lucemon: Satan Mode is the wrong Lucemon for any attack fixture — it prints
  "[When Attacking] … this Digimon unsuspends". The turn-loop tests use BT7-111 Lucemon: Chaos
  Mode, whose only clauses are play/digivolve-time.
