# EX10-020 Puppetmon — re-audit

Green, Lv.6 Mega, Virus, [Puppet]/[Dark Masters], play cost 11, DP 11000, no digivolution
requirements printed (`evoCosts: []`).

## Printed clauses

| # | Clause |
|---|---|
| C1 | `[Hand] [Main]` If you don't have any Digimon other than Digimon with [Dark Masters] in their texts, you may play this card with the play cost reduced by 5. At turn end, delete the Digimon this effect played. |
| C2 | `[On Play] [When Attacking]` Return 1 of your opponent's suspended Digimon to the bottom of the deck. |
| C3 | `[All Turns]` This Digimon can only digivolve into [Apocalymon]. |

## What the previous audit missed

The committed module IR was correct; the committed **test file was not evidence**. Three
problems, all fixed here:

1. **C3 was not tested at all.** The old `it("can only digivolve into Apocalymon")` built its
   own `ContinuousEffectLedger`, registered a hand-written predicate on it, and called
   `validateDigivolve` with a hand-written `deps` object. It proved the ledger primitive, not
   that EX10-020 registers a constraint. Deleting the whole `RestrictDigivolveInto` effect
   from the module left that test green. It also reached `engine.effects.continuous` and
   `engine/actions/digivolve`, both outside the test seam the brief requires.
2. **C2 (`[On Play]`) and C4 were proved only by injected timing** — `advance().fireForPermanent(OnPlay, …)`
   and `advance().verb.deletePermanent(…)`. Zero behavioural credit under the brief.
3. **C1's turn-end deletion was proved by `advance().fireGlobal(OnEndTurn)`** with
   `s.state.turnSeat` written by hand, not by the real turn loop.

Missing coverage: Q5062 (no Digimon at all), Q5057 (text-only match vs trait/name), Q6511
(security Digimon battles after the effect), the "own suspended Digimon is not a target"
boundary, and the face-down-green boundary on C4.

## Fixtures and technique notes

- C4's two negatives originally hung silently: with only one suspended opposing Digimon,
  `[When Attacking]` returns the battle target itself and no battle ever happens, so
  Puppetmon was never deleted. Every C4 test now seats a decoy suspended Digimon and steers
  the return onto it with `preferInstanceIds`, so the 20000 DP wall survives to win the
  battle. That is also what makes the deletion a production battle deletion rather than an
  injected verb.
- `advance().waitForMainPhase(1)` is not reachable in these one-sided fixtures (seat 1 parks
  in Draw with an empty deck), so the turn-boundary assertions use
  `settleAcrossTimers(() => s.state.turnSeat === 1)`.
- BT12-057 Quartzmon is the C3 negative: Lv.7 with a legal `{ Green, 6, cost 6 }` evolution
  requirement, asserted in the test, so the rejection is the name restriction and nothing else.

## Turn-end ordering proof (Q5063 / Q5736)

Board: EX10-020 is played by its own `[Hand] [Main]` effect (memory 6 -> 0, so the delayed
delete is armed on the permanent it played), then digivolves into BT15-102 Apocalymon in the
SAME turn through the public `digivolve` intent (stack asserted `["EX10-020"]`). BT15-102
prints `[End of Your Turn] [Once Per Turn] By placing 1 level 6 or lower card from your trash
as this Digimon's bottom digivolution card, activate 1 [On Play] effect on that card … Then,
trash the top 2 cards of your opponent's deck for each of this Digimon's level 6 digivolution
cards.` So one permanent carries two same-controller turn-end processings.

With `autoOrderTriggers: false` the harness leaves the decision pending. Both tests assert it
before answering:

- `s.state.pendingDecision.kind === "orderTriggers"`, raised to **seat 0** (the turn player).
- `triggerKeys` has 2 entries, `triggerTimings` is `["OnEndTurn", "OnEndTurn"]`.
- One key is the delayed delete (`…::subtrigger/0/[End of Your Turn] Delete this Digimon
  (delayed-delete-played).`), the other is Apocalymon's own IR effect (`…::BT15-102/ir-3-0`).

Order-dependent endpoint (the fixture's only source of opponent-side movement):

| Answer | Opponent trash | Opponent deck | Own battle area |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apocalymon key first | `["BT1-013", "BT1-014"]` | 1 (4 seeded - 2 trashed - 1 drawn) | empty |
| Delete key first | empty | 3 (4 seeded - 1 drawn) | empty |

Choosing the delete first removes the permanent before its `[End of Your Turn]` resolves, so
the effect never places the trash fodder and never trashes the opponent's deck. The engine
therefore honors the player's order rather than a fixed one.

Observation (not a defect): `triggerCardIds` reports `["BT15-102", "BT15-102"]`. The delayed
delete is anchored to the permanent, whose top card is now Apocalymon, so the decision labels
both entries with the current top card. The effect key text still names the delete
unambiguously, and a UI would render two distinct rows, but a client that labels rows only by
`triggerCardIds` would show the same card twice. Worth a coordinator note on the
`orderTriggers` payload.

## Catalog discrepancy (report only, not edited)

`cards.json` stores a NO-BREAK SPACE (U+00A0) instead of a normal space after the bracket in
both texts:

- `effectText` offset 82: `…with [Dark Masters] in their texts…`
- `securityEffectText` offset 93: `…with [Dark Masters] in its text…`

Harmless for this card (no filter reads the raw string here), but a `textContains`/`match:"text"`
filter on another card that searches for the literal `"[Dark Masters] in"` would miss it. The
catalog assertion in the test carries the U+00A0 so the discrepancy is pinned, not hidden.
