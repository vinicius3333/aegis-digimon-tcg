## Coordinator trap check: substring over-match

The printed gate is "with the [Mineral] or [Rock] trait", which is exact. The module uses
`match: "trait"` in all three places (cost filter, target filter, inherited `hostFilter`) and
never a `texts` substring. The negative is proved, not asserted: BT2-011 Vorvomon and
BT2-014 Lavorvomon carry the trait `Rock Dragon`, which contains "Rock" as a substring. They
appear as a target candidate, as a digivolution card offered to the cost, and as an inherited
host, and are refused in all three roles.

Mutation proof: flipping every `match: "trait"` to `match: "traitContains"` in the module
turns three behavioural tests red plus the IR test
(`docs/audits/EX10-reaudit/logs/EX10-028-mutation-traitContains.log`). The module was
restored byte-for-byte from `git show HEAD` afterwards.

## Round 2: what changed

Two Peer/stack gaps from round 1 were closed, and both existing routes were kept:

1. **The inherited carrier is now built through public intents.** `inherited: on a carrier stack
   built by playCard + digivolve…` plays Landramon from hand with the `playCard` intent (its own
   `[On Play]` cost is unpayable on an empty board, which is asserted), then digivolves BT10-064
   Gogmamon onto that permanent with the `digivolve` intent for its printed Black Lv.4 EvoCost 3,
   asserting the intermediate stack (`topCard` = Gogmamon, `stack` = [Landramon], DP 8000, memory
   0, the bonus draw in hand). A second Landramon is then played and its `[On Play]` trash cost
   takes the buried card out of that real stack. Endpoints asserted after settle: stack empty,
   trash holds exactly the buried instance, the opponent's play cost 4 Digimon is gone and the
   play cost 5 one survives (it is named first in `preferInstanceIds`, so a wrong ceiling would
   take it), the [Rock] host carries +3000 DP, ＜Reboot＞ and ＜Blocker＞, memory 0, exact hand,
   no `pendingDecision`.

2. **The inherited watcher is proved for an opponent-driven trash.** `inherited: an opponent's
   BT1-099 Hearts Attack trash…` has seat 1 play BT1-099 Hearts Attack ([Main] Trash all
   digivolution cards under 1 of your opponent's Digimon) through the public `playCard` intent.
   That is a genuine opponent effect: `runTrashDigivolution` passes `byEffectSeat: 1` into
   `trashDigivolutionCards`, which fires `onDigivolutionCardsDiscardedBatch`. Landramon's watcher
   fires against its own caster and deletes seat 1's play cost 4 Digimon while the play cost 5 one
   (named first in the preference list) survives. BT1-042 LoaderLeomon is on seat 1's board only
   to satisfy the blue Option's colour requirement; it is textless and play cost 7, so it never
   enters the delete pool.

### ＜De-Digivolve＞ cannot reach this watcher (and should not)

The brief suggested EX8-049 Golemon or EX7-046 Jazarichmon as the opponent-driven trash. Neither
works, for a reason that is correct rather than a gap. `peelStackTops`
(`apps/api/src/engine/effects/primitives.ts`) trashes the permanent's **top card** and promotes the
card beneath it; it fires `whenDigimonTopTrashed`, never
`onDigivolutionCardsDiscardedBatch`. That matches the printed rule: a ＜De-Digivolve＞-trashed card
was the Digimon itself, not one of its digivolution cards, so Landramon's "when effects trash this
card from a … Digimon's digivolution cards" does not apply. BT1-099 is the right shape of probe.

### Q5100 and the play-cost reduction

A catalog card that reduces an opponent's play cost does exist: **BT16-060 Tankdramon**
("… reduce the play costs of all of your opponent's Digimon by 1 until the end of their turn"),
compiled as a `CostModifier` with `mode: "reduce"`, `costType: "play"`, `controller: "opponent"`.
It was not used as the Q5100 probe: the same `[On Play]` also carries its own
`Delete … playCostLte: 4` against the opponent, resolving in the same window as the reduction it
creates, so a board that ends with one fewer opposing Digimon cannot be attributed to Landramon's
inherited delete rather than Tankdramon's own. The seeded
`advance(engine).ledgers.modifiers.addPlayCostAdjustment` isolates the boundary instead, and the
trash that fires the watcher in that test is still a public `[On Play]` cost. **The ledger arming
is therefore the only clean route to the Q5100 boundary in this catalog**, and it stays inside the
test seam.
