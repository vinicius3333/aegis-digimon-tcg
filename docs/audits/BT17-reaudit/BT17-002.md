The added test spends the use, refuses the second stack play in the same turn, then runs a real seat-0 turn and a real seat-1 turn through `advance().runTurn` (with the hand-laid `turnSeat`/memory chaining the harness requires) and proves the third stack play draws again.

## Peer/stack proof (added)

`carries the inherited draw through the real Digi-Egg route: hatch -> digivolve -> battle area`
walks the whole Digi-Egg lifecycle with public intents only, under the production turn loop:
`hatchEgg` -> `digivolve` the Blue Lv.3 BT1-028 onto the egg inside the breeding area (Lv.2 Blue,
cost 0, egg asserted as the stack) -> `moveFromBreeding` on the next own turn (carrier top card
BT1-028, stack exactly the hatched egg instance).

The comparative case is the card-kind boundary, taken on the same carrier in the same turn: a
Tamer (BT1-085) played out of the carrier's digivolution cards draws nothing, and a Digimon
(BT1-010) played out of the same digivolution cards draws exactly one. Note the printed text is
"one of your Digimon", not "this Digimon's", so a peer permanent WITHOUT BT17-002 beneath is not a
near miss — a stack play anywhere on your board legitimately draws. That was verified while
building this test and is why the kind boundary is the comparative used.
