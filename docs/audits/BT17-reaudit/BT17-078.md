# BT17-078 Omnimon (re-audit, lane 58)

Digimon, Lv7, White, DP 15000, playCost 9. ACE (overflowMemory 5).

## Printed clauses

1. `[Hand] [Counter] ＜Blast DNA Digivolve ([WarGreymon] + [MetalGarurumon])＞` — a field
   [WarGreymon] plus a hand [MetalGarurumon] DNA digivolve into Omnimon during a Counter
   window, no cost.
2. `＜Raid＞` — engine keyword (§16-23).
3. `＜Blocker＞` — engine keyword.
4. `[On Play] [When Digivolving] If DNA Digivolving, choose 1 opponent's Digimon and return the
   chosen Digimon and all of your opponent's Digimon with the same level to the bottom of the
   deck. Then, delete 1 of your opponent's Digimon.`

No inherited effect.

## Q&A coverage (Q2369, Q2851, Q6006)

- **Q2851** (delete resolves even when the "if DNA Digivolving" branch does not): covered by
  `deletes one opposing Digimon after a natural non-DNA play and returns nothing`. Hard-played
  Omnimon deletes exactly one opponent Digimon; nothing is returned; deck stays empty.
- **Q6006** (Omnimon removed mid-return; the "Then delete" still resolves): **documented
  untestable in-lane.** Reproducing it needs an opponent card carrying a "when [your Digimon]
  would leave" immediate effect that removes Omnimon during the first (return) step; that is a
  fixture involving a second unaudited card and the trigger-ordering seam, out of scope for a
  single-card lane. The IR already models the guarantee: the `Delete` action (effect[3]/[4]
  actions[2]) is a sibling of the `Return`, unconditional, so it runs regardless of the source's
  fate — asserted structurally by `keeps the post-return deletion independent of the DNA
  condition`.
- **Q2369** (＜Overflow＞ processed when a card carrying Omnimon ACE in its digivolution cards is
  trashed by this effect): **documented untestable in-lane.** ＜Overflow＞ is an engine ACE
  keyword driven by `overflowMemory`, not by this card's compiled IR, and the scenario places
  Omnimon under a third card. It belongs to an Overflow/ACE engine test, not this module.

## Defect fixed in the shared package (lane S)

`rejects a near-name (BlackWarGreymon) that only substring-matches the recipe` — now **green**
(the `it.fails` was flipped to `it`).

- File/function: `packages/shared/src/effects/data.ts`,
  `DNA_DIGIVOLUTION_REQUIREMENT_OVERRIDES["BT17-078"]`, consumed by `matchingDnaDigivolveCost`
  (`apps/api/src/engine/effects/primitives.ts` -> `dnaMaterialSpecMatches`).
- Was: the override stored the printed brackets as substring `names`, so BlackWarGreymon
  (BT2-112) and the X-Antibody variants were wrongly admitted as Blast DNA materials at cost 0.
- Now: `materials: [{ namesExact: ["WarGreymon"] }, { namesExact: ["MetalGarurumon"] }]`,
  mirroring the BT13-059 precedent in the same file. BlackWarGreymon + MetalGarurumon is
  refused with `invalid-evolution`.
- Red then green: see `docs/audits/BT17-reaudit/SHARED-NAME-ALIAS-MECHANISM.md`.

## Secondary observation (not a retained red)

BT17-078 prints no Main-phase `[DNA Digivolve]` header; its DNA is `[Hand][Counter]` Blast only
(§16-31). Because the shared override registers a plain `dnaDigivolveRequirement`, the
Main-phase `dnaDigivolve` verb also accepts the WarGreymon+MetalGarurumon pair outside a Counter
window. This may be an over-admission of route/timing. I did not encode it as a red assertion —
I have no ruling that explicitly forbids the Main-phase declaration, and the behavioural route
coverage here uses the unambiguous Counter path instead. Flagging for the coordinator.
