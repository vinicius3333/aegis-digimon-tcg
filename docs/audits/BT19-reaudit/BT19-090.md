## Defects fixed locally

- `BT19-090.ts`: both branch-B cost names used `match: "name"` (substring) for
  printed bracketed references. `[Shoutmon EX6]` and `[ShootingStarmon]` are
  exact-name gates, so both are now `match: "nameExact"`. **The IR changed:
  `effects.json` needs a resync.**
- `BT19-090.test.ts` was rewritten. The old file was almost entirely a mock
  harness — hand-rolled `CardDefinition`, `Permanent`, `EffectContext` and
  `Primitives` recorders calling `module.effectsForTiming(...).resolve(ctx)` —
  which proves the module's own call shape and nothing about the engine. Its one
  real-engine test seeded `deck: ["BT1-001"]`, a **Digi-Egg in the main deck**,
  which the fixture rules forbid.

## Honest limit on the `nameExact` proof

No card in the catalog has a name that *contains* "Shoutmon EX6" or
"ShootingStarmon" as a proper substring, so no fixture can make the substring
and exact matchers disagree. `BT9-035 Starmon` fails under both. The change is
therefore correct by specification and confirmed non-regressive by the positive
tests, but the near-miss test is not by itself a discriminator — stated here
rather than claimed as behavioural proof.

## Remaining gaps

- Branch B's "attack a player with 1 of your Digimon" is proved with a biased
  single-choice selection; no test asserts that the just-unsuspended EX6 or
  ShootingStarmon may itself be the attacker (both are legal candidates, and the
  filter is the unqualified "1 of your Digimon").
