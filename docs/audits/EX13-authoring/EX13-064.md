Digimon, Purple/Black, Lv.6 Mega, Virus, [Holy Warrior]/[Royal Knight]/[CS], 12000 DP,
play cost 12. Printed EvoCosts: Purple Lv.5 for 4 and Black Lv.5 for 4. SR.

4. `[Your Turn] All of your [Knightmon] text Digimon gain ＜Alliance＞ (...) and ＜Piercing＞`
5. `[Your Turn] [Once Per Turn] When any of your other Digimon or Tamers are played, 1 of your
   [Knightmon] text Digimon may gain ＜Rush＞ and ＜Collision＞ for the turn and attack.`

## KB check

`node tools/kb/query.mjs card EX13-064` → "(no knowledge-base entries)". None — EX13 is
pre-release. General rules consulted in `data/kb/rules/comprehensive.md`:

- §4-23-1 / §4-23-3 cards "with XX in their texts": the token anywhere in the information
  printed on the card, and an INHERITED-effect mention counts even when the card is not in
  digivolution cards. This is what makes `match: "text"` (not `"name"`) the correct encoding
  and BT7-059 DeadlyAxemon a legal target and grant recipient.
- §4-23-5 notes are not text: this card's own ＜Alliance＞ parenthetical names
  ＜Security A. +1＞, which does NOT make it a card with ＜Security A.＞ in its text. Nothing
  here filters on that token, so the rule only bounds what may be claimed.
- §16-24 ＜Alliance＞ and §16-30 ＜Collision＞ — the printed parenthetical on clause 4 restates
  §16-24 verbatim, so the grant is the bare keyword.
- §3-4-5-8 breeding-area cards cannot be referenced by effects, which keeps the keyword grants
  battle-area scoped without an explicit `zone`.

## Remaining ambiguity / declarative-only fields

- `excludeSelf: true` on clause 5's `sourceFilter` ("your OTHER Digimon or Tamers") is asserted
  structurally only. Deleting it reds nothing: the only behaviour it changes is the host
  reacting to its own play, and the subscription is installed as the permanent enters, so no
  fixture can distinguish the two readings today. EX3-054 / BT17-044 carry the same field for
  the same printed word.
- The clause-5 `Attack` is deliberately left unqualified (no `attackPlayer` /
  `attackPlayerOnly`), which is the printed reading and keeps every legal opposing Digimon plus
  the player in `forceAttack`'s candidate list. Only the structural assertion proves it:
  `forceAttack` always lists the player first and the harness answers the target prompt by
  candidate order, so a test cannot steer the forced attack onto a Digimon.
- ＜Piercing＞ does not surface through `observe().hasKeyword` — a continuously granted Piercing
  lands in the battle-modifier ledger that `observe().hasPierce` reads. Noted here because the
  obvious probe silently reports `false`; the behavioural proof uses `hasPierce` plus a real
  battle.
