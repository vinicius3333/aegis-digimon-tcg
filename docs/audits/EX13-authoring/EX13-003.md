Printed inherited text (the only clause on the card):

> [Your Turn] [Once Per Turn] When your security stack is removed from, this
> Digimon may digivolve into a Digimon card with [Kentaurosmon] in its name or
> the [Holy Beast] trait in the hand with the cost reduced by 1.

Clause decomposition:

1. `[Your Turn]` — active only on the controller's turn.
2. `[Once Per Turn]` — one resolution per turn, resetting on the next own turn.
3. `When your security stack is removed from` — own seat only, not the opponent's.
4. `this Digimon may digivolve` — the carrier itself, optional.
5. destination `with [Kentaurosmon] in its name` — substring name match.
6. destination `or the [Holy Beast] trait` — exact trait match.
7. `in the hand` — source zone hand.
8. `with the cost reduced by 1` — normal digivolution requirements, cost paid minus 1.

Notes on the behavioural routes:

- **Public own-security removal on your own turn.** The public route is
  `AD1-017` Dynasmon's `[On Play] By trashing your top or bottom security card`,
  played by the controller on their own turn (its cost drops 11 → 6 with four
  `[Lucemon]`/`[Witchelny]` text cards in the trash). This is the same card
  `BT21-001.test.ts` uses for its own-seat negative, so the event is known to
  fire for the playing seat.
- **Trait discrimination.** The hand holds `BT1-051` Reppamon (`[Holy Beast]`,
  matching), `BT12-036` Mikemon (`[Beast]`, near-matching but not equal), and
  `BT9-035` Starmon (`[Mutant]`, non-matching). Only Reppamon is taken; the other
  two are asserted still in hand, so the filter discriminates.
- **Name branch is genuinely separate.** No printed Kentaurosmon carries the
  `[Holy Beast]` trait (they are `[Holy Warrior]`/`[Royal Knight]`), so the name
  branch cannot be satisfied by the trait branch. `BT3-043` Kentaurosmon (Lv.6,
  Yellow, cost 3) digivolves from the Lv.5 `BT13-041` Chirinmon carrier for 2.
- **Exact endpoints.** Both positive tests assert top-card instance identity,
  the full `stack` beneath it (`EX13-003` retained as a source), security length
  and faces, hand contents including the digivolution bonus draw, exact memory
  (10 − 6 − 1 = 3 and 10 − 6 − 2 = 2) and `pendingDecision === undefined`.
- **Once per turn.** Two Dynasmon plays in one turn remove security twice; the
  second removal does not evolve (carrier still `BT1-051`, the second
  destination still in hand, memory only down by the play cost). The turn loop
  then advances to the next own turn, where the effect resolves again into
  `BT1-058` Chirinmon with the stack `["EX13-003", "BT1-046", "BT1-051"]`.
- **Evolution stack.** The smallest legal stack reaching the carrier is built
  publicly: `hatchEgg` → breeding digivolve into `BT1-046` Kudamon (Lv.3 Yellow
  from Lv.2 Yellow) → `moveFromBreeding`. The egg instance id is tracked across
  all three transitions and the inherited clause still fires from the promoted
  permanent.

## Remaining ambiguity

- `[Your Turn]` negative uses the opponent's attack on the controller's security
  during a real opponent turn. The destination is legal and in hand, so the
  refusal is attributable to the turn restriction, but on the opponent's turn the
  controller also has no memory to spend, so that test is weaker evidence than
  the own-seat negative (which runs on the controller's own turn with ample
  memory and only fails the seat condition).
- Whether "the cost reduced by 1" on an own-security trigger interacts with
  other cost reductions is unanswerable until the KB gains EX13 rulings.
