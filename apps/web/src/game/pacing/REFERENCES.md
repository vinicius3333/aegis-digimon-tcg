# TCG effect pacing: references

This file collects citable evidence on how digital card games pace chained effects, opponent actions, and history. It maps that evidence to the Aegis sequential-chain knobs so we can tune them with sources, not guesses.

These sources justify the presentation principles. They do not prescribe TCG timing values
or prove that a player can read a full card clause in a short beat. The harness measures a
headline reading floor, keeps the recent text stack available, and separately reports full
reading time. The historical survey below contains secondary reports and explicitly labeled
estimates; it is background, not a specification for the new implementation.

What this means for us (estimates, to verify with the harness):

- Our Normal sequential benchmark beats (720 ms focus + 700 ms announce + 300 ms settle, about 1.72 s plus results) are already slower per effect than DCGO (about 0.95 s plus results).
- DCGO gets readability from text that persists and stacks while the board moves on, not from long blocking beats.
- DCGO keeps cause before result. We must keep it too.
- DCGO coalesces repeated DP pulses on one card. Grouping repeated effects (×N) goes further.

## 3. Takeaways for Aegis

1. **Minimum readable time for an effect clause (announceMs, announceMaxMs).**
   Rule: `readableMs = baseMs + words / wps * 1000`.
   Use the BBC mixed-audience rate, 180 wpm = 3 words/s (cited), not the Brysbaert mean of ~4 words/s, because card text is dense and players also scan the board.
   Suggested `baseMs = 360` (reuse sourceHoldMs as the orient time; estimate).
   Examples (estimate): 10 words -> 3.7 s; 20 words -> 7.0 s; 25 words -> 8.7 s (clamped by announceMaxMs 8000).
   The current announceMs 700 covers only ~1 word at 3 words/s. Treat 700 ms as the floor for a known or repeated clause, and apply the formula to the first view of an opponent's clause.
2. **Effect speed must not shrink reading time below the floor (Effect speed scale).** Fast 0.55 x 700 = 385 ms, which is near NN/g's "drag" line for motion but far below any reading rate. Scale motion beats (sourceHoldMs, settleMs, results) by the setting. Clamp the clause beat at `max(announceMs x scale, readableMs x 0.55)` or similar (estimate). WCAG 2.2.1 asks for a range of at least 10x. Slow 1.4 / Fast 0.55 spans ~2.5x. Add a "wait for click" mode for opponent chains to satisfy the spirit of 2.2.1.
3. **settleMs 300, minorSettleMs 150, sourceHoldMs 360 sit inside guidance.** NN/g gives 100-400 ms, Material medium tokens 250-400 ms (cited). Keep them. Snap them to 50 ms token steps if we adopt Material tokens (e.g. 350 ms).
4. **Minor effects can go faster (minorAnnounceMs 450).** Hearthstone cut trigger animations from 0.8 s to 0.2 s and flagged some at 0 s (cited). For memory/DP-only effects, try ~200-300 ms with no standalone clause, and show the delta on the number itself (estimate). Recent clauses remain available in the scrollable toast column.
5. **Never block input on visuals (unitBudgetMs, budgetCeilingMs).** Gwent decoupled logic from visuals twice (4.1, 9.0) so players do not wait (cited). Hearthstone adds "slush" time when animations eat a turn; Pocket players complain when they do not (cited). Pause the player's decision timer while a chain animates, or refund it. budgetCeilingMs 20000 equals Hearthstone's ~20 s rope window (estimate that this is a useful cap). A click or key should skip to the end of the current beat.
6. **Bot delay should follow the chain, not overlap it (bot delay 2000-2800 ms).** Start the bot's think timer after the last settle, not after the server event. After a long chain, the player has already had watch time, so a shorter delay (~1000-1500 ms) keeps turns moving (estimate; no source gives a number for bot pacing).
7. **Resolution strip: show owner and origin (strip, minChainLength 2).** LoR 3.6 marks which player started the stack; Hearthstone borders history blue/red; Arena lets you inspect any stack item (cited). Color strip chips by owner, number them in resolution order, and allow hover/long-press for full text. minChainLength 2 matches how Snap and Arena only show ordering when more than one item exists (estimate).
8. **Notices: size lifetime to text and allow pause (effect-clause notices).** Material's snackbar range is 4-10 s (cited). Replace the fixed notice lifetime with `max(4000, readableMs)` capped at 10000 (estimate). Pause the timer on hover or focus. WCAG 2.2.2's 5 s rule covers moving content; content that animates or auto-updates for > 5 s needs a pause or dismiss control (cited). These are research recommendations, not implemented controls.
9. **Persistent history is an alternative, not the selected UI.** Hearthstone keeps ~7-10 actions; Master Duel and LoR keep full replays (cited). The requested Aegis layout uses recent scrollable toasts and omits the last-chain recap and effect-history panel.
10. **Respect reduced motion.** WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) and Shadowverse's "simple card effects" option both point to a low-motion path (cited). Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, drop glow and travel animation, keep the text beat and the strip.

11. **DCGO is the baseline (all watched beats).** DCGO spends about 0.95 s per field effect before its results and keeps each clause on screen for 5.5 s in a stacked list (cited, source code). Keep blocking beats near DCGO's total, and let the scrollable toast stack carry the text afterwards, instead of stretching the blocking beat to the full reading time.

12. **The `stacked` pacing style follows takeaway 11.** It keeps DCGO's shape: a brief active beat (720 ms focus + 200 ms announce and 100 ms settle at Normal) and earlier clauses that stay in the scrollable column, dimmed, for 5 s from when they appeared (DCGO: 5.5 s), at most five under the one resolving now. The 920 ms field lead-in approximates DCGO's roughly 0.9 s before results; reducing announcement and settle waits preserves existing card travel and result animation durations. Readable time counts the active beat plus the dimmed time, at 3 words/s. The harness compares it with `sequential` on every scenario and speed (`apps/web/test/pacing/`).

13. **Long chains speed up; a floor keeps them readable.** The earlier harness tuning used short minor/repeated beats, 40% beats after the second effect, and a 150 ms beat after an answer. The current reading floor is 1.72 s at Normal: 360 ms orientation plus 4 words at 3 words/s, with frame sampling margin. Those are headline and continuity budgets, not time to read a full clause. Use the current committed `test/pacing/pacing-baseline.json` for reproducible scenario durations; historical per-effect averages do not establish current performance after the acceptance protocol and flight changes.
