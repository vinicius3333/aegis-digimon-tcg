## Scoring model

Each card receives exactly five two-point components:

1. **Catalog/rules** — catalog identity/text and applicable KB rulings.
2. **IR trace** — direct compiled IR, triggers/actions/requirements, and exclusive registration.
3. **Behavioral proof** — feasible natural public play, evolution, attack, security, turn, or leave-play origins in source tests. Structural assertions do not receive this credit.
4. **Peer and stack proof** — peer implementation comparison and legal stack/target coverage.
5. **Executed delivery gates** — fixed at 0/2 by the task boundary.

## Card findings and source proof

### BT18-081 Rhihimon

- The direct IR represents the hand-only Main effect, exact `Loweemon` plus exact `KaiserLeomon` trash costs, cost 3, ignored evolution requirements, Jamming, optional inherited-effect Tamer play, and inherited once-per-turn -4000 DP attack modifier.
- KB Q3038/Q3039 confirm hand-only activation and the requirement for both named materials. Q3040 confirms that a Tamer digivolving as-is does not fire Digimon-only triggers; Q3041/Q3042 cover evolution bonus draw and same-turn attack timing. Q6662–Q6664 cover Tamer cards in stacks and inherited versus non-inherited text.
- A real host-binding gap was corrected: the first material placement binds `rhihimonHost`, the second material uses that bound host, and the final evolution uses `fromSelectionRef`. Both material filters now use `match: "nameExact"`, so only the bracketed card names qualify while the second placement remains attached to the bound host. This prevents the two materials/evolution from splitting across multiple legal Tamers. The test activates the hand effect through a public intent with two legal Tamers, both exact materials, and a natural inherited-Tamer play.
- Peer comparison with BT15-091 and BT17-026 confirms the same bound-host compound-cost pattern. The legal stack is a purple/yellow Tamer plus the two named Hybrid materials.

### BT18-082 Lucemon: Chaos Mode

- The direct IR represents both On Play and When Digivolving opponent-choice deletions as `upTo: true` targets with `chooser: "opponent"`; there is no action-level optional prompt or controller shorthand. It also represents the Recovery +1 and opponent top-security trash fallback guarded by `ifThisEffectDidNotDelete`, and the once-per-turn bottom-security leave-play replacement.
- KB Q3043 confirms that the opponent's choice and the post-clause fallback are evaluated after the choice resolves. The direct replacement explicitly targets the owner's security bottom card.
- Source proof uses natural public play with a real seat-1 response: seat 0's attempted response is rejected, while seat 1 can decline or select the deletion target. The same public flow covers both fallback and deletion branches, plus natural evolution from exact-name Lucemon; a BT18-086 Larva alternate-route negative proves a Lucemon variant cannot satisfy `[Lucemon]`. An opponent Millenniummon play still causes the leave-play replacement. These cover both branches without direct timing/event injection.
- Peer evidence includes the shared `ifThisEffectDidNotDelete` action pattern and the existing BT18 bottom-security source assertion. No unresolved card-specific seam remains.

### BT18-083 LordKnightmon

- The direct IR represents hand Counter Blast Digivolve, the optional level-5-or-lower Digimon with `[Knightmon]` in its text from hand/trash, and the global DP-relative Collision aura.
- The catalog says “All Digimon,” not “your Digimon.” The previous `controllerDefault: "mine"` scope was corrected to `controllerDefault: "any"`; the natural source proof verifies both players' qualifying targets and excludes both players' targets above the source DP.
- Peer comparison with global aura implementations such as BT22-076 establishes `controllerDefault: "any"` for an unqualified “all Digimon” clause. The test uses a live LordKnightmon source, legal low-DP targets on both sides, and high-DP negative controls.

### BT18-084 AncientSphinxmon

- The direct IR represents On Play/When Digivolving deletion of one opponent unsuspended Digimon, the optional own-stack level-4-or-lower Dark Animal/Mythical Beast/Hybrid play replacement, and the printed DigiXros alternatives.
- The DigiXros recipe was corrected from a duplicate Loweemon slot to two distinct slots: `[Loweemon]` or `[Duskmon]` plus `[KaiserLeomon]` or `[Velgrmon]`. The replacement target was also corrected to `zone: "digivolutionCards"` with `hostFilter: { sourceRef: "triggerSubject" }`, so it can use only this AncientSphinxmon's own stack.
- Natural source proof covers legal Duskmon/Velgrmon DigiXros, rejection of two materials from one slot, On Play unsuspended targeting, and an opponent deletion that naturally plays a qualifying own-stack source.
- Peer/stack comparison follows BT18-017/BT18-028's distinct DigiXros-slot behavior and BT17-084's own-stack `hostFilter` pattern.

### BT18-085 Zanbamon

- The direct IR represents reduction only when one of your Digimon would digivolve into exact Zanbamon, with one reduction per opponent-trash color, and the Your Turn Security Attack +1/+2000 DP scaling for every two colors.
- The natural test publicly digivolves a legal purple level-5 source with four distinct opponent-trash colors, proving the cost reduction, resulting DP, and Security Attack amount. The existing runtime source proof also covers the live scaling behavior.
- Peer comparison uses the shared color-scaling path and BT18 Hybrid/Warrior stack fixtures. The exact Zanbamon `nameExact` target prevents unrelated evolutions from receiving the reduction.

### BT18-086 Lucemon: Larva

- The direct IR represents Security play of one exact-name Lucemon from trash, the Breeding-only all-turns Satan Mode leave-play replacement, and the all-turns deletion restriction while a non-white Lucemon substring is present. The separate aura condition intentionally remains `match: "name"` because its text is `[Lucemon] in its name` rather than an exact bracketed card reference.
- KB Q3044 confirms that the protection applies to all own 0 DP Digimon, including deletion through effects/rules. Q6242 confirms that a security Larva effect resolves during a security check. The missing `dp: { op: "eq", value: 0 }` target condition was corrected.
- Natural source proof uses an opponent attack to reveal Larva and play exact-name Lucemon from trash, plus a variant-only trash negative proving Chaos Mode is not played. It also uses an opponent Millenniummon deletion to distinguish protected 0 DP from normal Digimon, and an opponent deletion of Satan Mode that moves Larva from Breeding to the battle area. The security assertion correctly observes Lucemon entering play; Larva itself is subsequently removed from security as the checked card.
- Peer review follows the shared Security timing, Breeding replacement, and effect-restriction paths. No unresolved card-specific seam remains.

### BT18-087 Owen Dreadnought

- The direct IR represents conditional start-of-turn memory set, the all-turns watcher for a card removed from the opponent's security stack with a suspend-self cost and 4000 DP boundary, and Security self-play.
- Peer comparison with BT15-013 confirms `whenSecurityRemoved` plus `sourceFilter: { controller: "opponent" }`; the explicit removed-security-seat condition is consistent with the shared watcher gate.
- Source proof drives start-of-turn through a real `runOneTurn`, drives the watcher through an own attack that naturally removes the opponent's security, verifies the 4000/5000 boundary and self-suspension, and drives Security self-play through an opponent attack revealing Owen. No direct security/event injection remains in this card test.

### BT18-088 Takuya Kanbara & Koji Minamoto

- The placement host scope was corrected from any own Tamer to `underFilter: { isSelfRef: true }`, matching “under this Tamer.” The inherited attack now also carries a `selfHasTrait` condition for the printed Hybrid/Ten Warriors requirement. Natural proof uses a real turn with one other Tamer to place three distinct Hybrid cards, a security attack reveal, an eligible end-of-turn attack from a Hybrid host, and a non-Hybrid negative host.
- Peer stack evidence includes the BT18 Hybrid/Tamer stacks used by BT18-018 and the shared `PlaceUnder`/inherited `Attack` runtime paths.

### BT18-089 Tommy Himi
