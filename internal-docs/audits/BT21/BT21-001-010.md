1. Catalog/rules

## Rules and runtime findings applied across the range

- The local rules material treats a card being “in text” as including the name, traits, effects, inherited effects, and other defined text sources, not merely the visible main effect. This governs Gurimon's Gammamon-text check and the relevant matcher path (comprehensive rules §4-23 and KB Q4519).
- Stacked cards and digivolution cards retain the information needed for stack matching (comprehensive rules §§4-7 and 4-8). The `selfDigivolutionStackCountAtLeast` condition therefore counts matching cards in the source stack, while the legal stack still must satisfy each printed evolution requirement.
- Linking is a separate card/engine seam: a link card is not a field permanent and has one link location (comprehensive rules §§4-9–4-10). This matters to Swipemon's self-linked trigger and Gatchmon's linked Raid/link proof.
- Trigger effects are checked and processed in the relevant timing windows, with simultaneous security removal and trigger ordering covered by comprehensive rules §§15-5-2, 15-6, 15-8, and 15-16; KB Q4516–Q4518 and Q4520 provide the card-specific security-removal interpretation.
- Reveal/add/bottom processing follows the search/reveal rules in comprehensive rules §15-15-3. Gammamon's selectable Main digivolution is routed through the interpreter's effect timing seam, while Q5210 and Q6944 cover simultaneous Your Turn activations and the status of a card that is not yet in hand.
- `registerIrCard(cardId, compiled)` is the required executable registration path. Each audited module has one such registration and no direct duplicate `registerCard` registration. The registration runtime's internal compatibility call is an implementation seam, not a second registration in the card module.

## Card-by-card evidence

### BT21-001 — Gigimon

Catalog/rules evidence: The catalog record identifies a Red Digi-Egg, Lesser/LIBERATOR, with a `[Your Turn][Once Per Turn]` inherited effect that watches an opponent security stack being removed and may evolve one of this player's Digimon into a hand Digimon with Reptile or Dragonkin, reducing the cost by 1. KB Q4516–Q4518 establish the security-removal timing and that the follow-up evolution is checked after the security card has been removed.

IR trace: `apps/api/src/cards/BT21/BT21-001.ts` registers only `registerIrCard("BT21-001", compiled)`. The compiled effect uses inherited `YourTurn` and `OncePerTurn`, a `whenSecurityRemoved` sub-trigger with opponent source/controller filtering and an opponent removed-security-seat condition, then an optional hand Digivolve action targeting the Reptile/Dragonkin union with `payCost` and `reduceCost: 1`. The runtime seams in `effect.ts`, sub-trigger handling, conditions, and `actions/digivolve.ts` account for the trigger, seat, optional choice, target, and reduced cost.

Behavioral proof: `apps/api/src/cards/BT21/BT21-001.test.ts` contains structural assertions and natural public attack evidence that removes an opponent security card and checks the resulting Cyberdramon evolution and reduced memory cost, as well as own-security, decline, and once-per-turn boundaries. Those scenarios were not run here; manual timing/event fixtures remain supplemental.

Peer and stack proof: The source uses the same security-removal and effect-driven evolution seams as peer inherited-trigger cards, and the catalog provides valid Reptile/Dragonkin targets such as BT21-024 Cyberdramon over a legal red Lv4 stack. The direct module's opponent-source and removed-seat checks address the common security-controller failure mode.

Correction/limitation: No confirmed semantic defect remains in the direct module. The generated JSON snapshot lacks the explicit opponent source/fire-condition detail and is stale relative to the direct module; it was left read-only by instruction.
