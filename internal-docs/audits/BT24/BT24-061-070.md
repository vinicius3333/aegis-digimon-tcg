## Scope and method

This audit covers BT24-061 through BT24-070 in card-number order. The catalog source was checked against immutable blob `efbecc002fb9000789123e2f91f201466e1e5b0a`; the current `packages/shared/src/cards/data/cards.json` hashes to that blob. For every card I compared the catalog and printed rules text with the direct TypeScript module, compiled IR, shared IR action definitions, relevant interpreter paths, source-level behavioral tests, nearby peers, stack semantics, registration, and the rules knowledge base.

The five scored components are exactly two points each:

1. Catalog/rules
2. IR trace
3. Behavioral proof
4. Peer and stack proof
5. Executed delivery gates

The requested delivery gates were intentionally not executed: no tests, typecheck, lint, formatter, browser/UI check, focused/mechanism/collection gate, or `git diff --check` was run. Consequently every card is provisional, every Executed delivery gates score is `0/2`, and no card can exceed `8/10` in this pass. Structural assertions alone and manually fired primitive events where a natural game origin exists were not counted as full Behavioral proof.

## Registration and runtime findings

BT24-061 through BT24-070 each register executable behavior exactly once with `registerIrCard(cardId, compiled)`. No target module contains a duplicate or legacy `registerCard` registration. No module registration required correction.

The compiled IR and interpreter trace confirmed the following important semantics:

- `RevealAdd` supports the specified reveal count, independent selection slots, free play, and top/bottom return ordering. BT24-066's second qualifying card uses `requiresMinRevealed: 2`, so it cannot select the same revealed card for both destinations.
- `source: "thisDigimon"` correctly scopes hosted digivolution-card selection. This preserves BT24-062's own-stack play and BT24-065's replacement play from this Diaboromon's stack; hand candidates remain in the owning seat's hand.
- `withSubTriggerFrequency` and `sharedUseKey` preserve once-per-turn scope. BT24-062's End of Attack and End of Opponent's Turn branches share one once-per-turn use, while BT24-065's replacement and BT24-067's linked Rei trigger retain their own timing/frequency.
- An omitted controller on BT24-064's suspension watcher intentionally observes either player's Digimon or Tamer. The direct module is correct; the generated `effects.json` snapshot still shows a stale `mine` default and was not edited.
- The `ifThisEffectDidNotAct` condition in BT24-069 correctly makes the opponent-deck trash conditional on the opponent declining the optional hand trash.
- BT24-065's exact `Diaboromon` name match and source anchor prevent unrelated named cards or unrelated stacks from satisfying the replacement. The KB rulings Q5644, Q5645, and Q5646 were checked for the Keramon interaction, simultaneous-leave behavior, and the played BT17-059 timing case.

The generated `packages/shared/src/effects/effects.json` snapshot was not treated as the source of executable behavior and was not edited. Its known stale differences include the BT24-062 restriction representation, BT24-064 controller default, BT24-065 name/source detail, BT24-066 Tamer-only filter, BT24-067 name/count representation, and BT24-069 raw conditional representation; the direct modules and runtime trace are authoritative for this audit.
